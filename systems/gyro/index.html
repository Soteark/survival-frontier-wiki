    Gyro Stabilizer Parts¶

    A set of parts for building your own self-levelling craft: hover platforms, flying machines, anything that has to stay flat. A spinning Gyro Flywheel in a gimbal gives you a steady reference. Angle Encoders measure how far your craft is tilted against it. Small signal parts shape that number, and Throttle Thrusters push by exactly the amount they're told. None of the parts knows what "level" is or how hard to correct. That comes from how you wire and tune them.

    Experimental

    The gyro parts are experimental. How they behave, and the numbers on this page, may change in later updates. Test new builds somewhere safe: a badly tuned controller or a heavy gimbal can make a craft swing, flip or fling itself.

    At a glance

    • Signal flow: Gyro Flywheel → Angle Encoders → Gain / Rate / Mixer / Flip / Clamp / Set Point → Throttle Thrusters. A Scope graphs any signal.
    • Flywheels come in 1x1 (~50 kg), 3x3 (~110 kg) and 5x5 (~250 kg). They need Inverter power and must hang in a gimbal.
    • Throttle Thruster: a signal of 0-100 is its thrust in percent. Full thrust is 8,000 N, enough to hold up about 800 kg.
    • Signals pass along their own kind of wire, and the whole chain reacts in the same tick.
    • How to get them: four Traveling Trader blueprints (Gyro Flywheels, Signal Parts, Throttle Thruster, Carbon Pipes), then craft at the Craftbot.

    Wiring and settings¶

    • Signals travel on their own kind of wire. Connect parts with the Connect Tool, the same as logic.
    • Every signal part adds up all the signals wired into it (up to 8), and can feed up to 32 other parts.
    • The whole chain reacts in the same tick the encoder sees a tilt, so there's no lag however long your chain is. If you wire a loop, the looped part reads its value from the tick before.
    • Look at any signal part to see its output and settings.
    • Use (E) steps a setting up, Tinker steps it down. Crouch to change a part's second setting.
    • Settings are saved with the part.

    The parts¶

    Gyro Flywheel (1x1, 3x3, 5x5)¶

    A heavy flywheel that spins on Inverter power and keeps its axle upright (whichever end is already up). It's your steady reference for Angle Encoders.

    1x1 3x3 5x5
    Size (blocks) 1 × 1 × 1 3 × 3 × 3 5 × 5 × 5
    Weight ~50 kg ~110 kg ~250 kg
    Good for small, light rings medium rings big or heavy rings
    Value
    Spin-up 6 s from stopped to full
    Coast-down 10 s once power stops
    Holding at full spin, cancels 60% of the gimbal's unwanted turning every tick
    Power 1 charge per 6 in-game hours of running (a quarter of a day), from an Inverter
    Wire from 1 Inverter (power)
    Wire into up to 4 Angle Encoders
    • It must hang in a gimbal. It only steadies the body it's built on. Hang it in two bearings at right angles, both horizontal, so the craft can tilt around it.
    • Keep the inner ring small. If the flywheel's body has more than 60 parts it gives up with "Can't hold this much - mount it on a small gimbal ring". Bolted straight onto a craft, it won't hold anything.
    • Light, balanced rings level fastest. Heavy rings make it slow. Past about 15 times its own weight, it warns "Rings too heavy for this flywheel - use a bigger flywheel or lighter rings". Bigger flywheels level faster in heavier rings.
    • Like a real gyro, it doesn't resist turning around its own axle.
    • Its stored charge only keeps it spinning while the Inverter is still wired in.

    Look at it to see its spin and how fast the gimbal is turning, plus a status: Holding steady, Spinning up, Levelling (with how far the axle is off upright), Needs power: wire an Inverter into it, or No power.

    Angle Encoder¶

    Outputs your craft's tilt in degrees, measured against a linked Gyro Flywheel. It measures twist around the direction its face points.

    • Use one per axis. Face it forwards to read roll, or sideways to read pitch. Don't point it straight up: that axis is the flywheel's own spin.
    • Build it on the craft, not on the gimbal, and wire the flywheel into it.
    • It sets itself to 0 when you first link it. Level the craft and press Use to set the current angle as level (0) any time.
    • Output runs from −180 to 180. Which way is positive follows the right-hand rule around its face direction. If a correction comes out backwards, add a Flip.

    Gain¶

    Multiplies its signal. This is how hard to correct.

    Setting Range Default
    Gain (Use = more, Tinker = less) ×0.05 to ×50, in preset steps ×1

    Rate¶

    Outputs how fast its signal is changing, per second. Feed it a tilt and it tells you how fast the craft is swinging. Add that in through a Gain to brake corrections before they overshoot.

    Setting Steps Default
    Smoothing (Use = smoother, Tinker = sharper) 0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, 90% 50%

    Mixer¶

    Adds together every signal wired into it. No settings.

    Flip¶

    Outputs minus its signal. The thruster on the opposite side of the craft needs the opposite correction. No settings.

    Clamp¶

    Caps its signal at ± a limit, and turns anything inside the dead zone into 0.

    Setting Steps Default
    Limit (Use / Tinker) ±1, 2, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 40, 50, 60, 75, 90, 100 ±100
    Dead zone (crouch + Use / Tinker) 0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 5 0

    Set Point¶

    Outputs a fixed number you choose, from −100 to 100. Use it as the base throttle your craft needs to hover. Nothing can be wired into it.

    Setting Step Default
    Value (Use = +, Tinker = −) 1, or 10 while crouching 0

    Throttle Thruster¶

    Pushes with exactly its signal as a percent of full thrust. It pushes away from its nozzle, so point the nozzle down to lift.

    Value
    Size 2 × 2 × 4 blocks
    Full thrust (signal 100) 8,000 N, enough to hold up about 800 kg
    Signal 0 to 100. Below 0 counts as 0, above 100 as 100
    Power about 1 charge per 30 s at full thrust, less at lower throttle, from an Inverter
    Wire from an Inverter, signal parts, switches (up to 8 in all)
    • Arming switch (optional): wire in a switch or other logic. With any switch wired in, at least one must be on or the thruster won't fire.
    • Look at it to see its throttle and status: Firing, Idle, Switched off, Needs power: wire an Inverter into it or No power.

    Scope¶

    Draws up to 3 signals as a live graph floating above itself. Use it to watch your controller wobble while you tune.

    • Wiring order sets the colour: first red, second green, third blue.
    • Shows the last 2.4 seconds, on a graph about 1.5 m wide and 1 m tall that turns to face you.
    • It scales itself to fit, but never zooms in tighter than ±10.

    Carbon Pipes¶

    Very light, paintable carbon pipes for building gimbal rings. They fit and snap like the small metal pipes: Straight, Long (4 blocks), Bend, T and Cross. Light, balanced rings let a flywheel level fast.

    Worked example: a self-levelling hover platform¶

    This build keeps a square platform flat on four thrusters, one at each corner.

    1. Power¶

    You need an Inverter fed from your batteries (see Power). In this build it powers 5 devices: the flywheel and four thrusters. Use an Inverter of a high enough level for that many outputs, or two Inverters.

    2. The gimbal¶

    1. Build a small inner ring of Carbon Pipes around a Gyro Flywheel with its axle pointing up, and fix the flywheel to it.
    2. Put the inner ring on a bearing inside a slightly bigger outer ring.
    3. Put the outer ring on a second bearing on the platform's frame, at right angles to the first. Both bearing axes must be horizontal.
    4. Wire the Inverter into the flywheel and wait about 6 seconds. It should say Holding steady.

    Tip

    Keep the flywheel and inner ring under 60 parts, and keep both rings light and balanced. If it says the rings are too heavy, use a bigger flywheel or fewer pipes.

    3. Sensing the tilt¶

    1. Build two Angle Encoders on the platform itself, not on the rings:
      • a pitch encoder facing sideways
      • a roll encoder facing forwards
    2. Wire the flywheel into both.
    3. Park the platform level and press Use on each encoder to set 0.

    4. The hover throttle¶

    Work out the throttle your platform needs to just hover. With four thrusters, each one holds about 800 kg at full:

    hover throttle ≈ platform weight ÷ (4 × 800 kg) × 100

    A 1,200 kg platform needs about 1,200 ÷ 3,200 × 100 ≈ 38. Build a Set Point and set it to 38.

    5. One correction per axis¶

    For pitch:

    1. Pitch encoder → Gain (start at ×2). This is the push back toward level.
    2. Pitch encoder → Rate → Gain (start at ×0.5). This brakes the swing so it doesn't overshoot.
    3. Both Gains → a Mixer. That Mixer's output is your pitch correction.
    4. Pitch correction → a Flip, for the other end of the platform.

    Build the same again for roll.

    6. The four thrusters¶

    Give each thruster its own Mixer (nozzle down, one at each corner), and wire in:

    Thruster Set Point Pitch Roll
    Front left ✓ pitch correction roll correction
    Front right ✓ pitch correction flipped roll
    Back left ✓ flipped pitch roll correction
    Back right ✓ flipped pitch flipped roll

    Wire each Mixer into its thruster, and the Inverter into every thruster.

    7. Test and tune¶

    1. Wire your Scope to the two encoders and one thruster's Mixer.
    2. Lift off and tip the platform a little. The encoder line should swing back to 0.
    3. Gets worse instead of better? That axis's correction is backwards. Swap which side gets the flipped signal (or add one more Flip on that axis).
    4. Slow to level? Raise the first Gain.
    5. Wobbles or overshoots? Raise the Gain after the Rate, or lower the first Gain.
    6. Twitchy at rest? Put a Clamp with a small dead zone after the encoder, or raise the Rate's smoothing.
    7. Sinks or climbs? Adjust the Set Point.

    Add an off switch

    Wire one switch into all four thrusters. While it's off, none of them fire, so the platform can sit on the ground with the controller live.
