    Fishing¶

    Cast a rod into a lake, a chemical pool or a pool of crude oil, wait for the bobber to dip, hook the bite and reel it in. There are 12 fish to find, including two legendaries. Which ones bite depends on the kind of water, how far you are from the crash site, the time of day, your rod and your bait. Big fish fight back on the line. Every so often you pull up junk or a Sunken Crate full of loot instead. Fish are filleted into Fish Fillets for the Frontier Kitchen, they can be handed in for Catch Orders, and the party's best catches go into the Fish Journal.

    At a glance

    • Rods: Fishing Rod (Workbench or Craftbot, known from the start), Carbon Rod (Craftbot, Traveling Trader blueprint), Frontier Rod (Catch Order reward only).
    • Water: lakes and ponds, chemical pools, crude oil. Each kind has its own fish and junk.
    • Distance bands from the crash site: near (under 600 m), mid (600-2,500 m), far (2,500 m and more).
    • Time windows: day, night, and dawn/dusk.
    • Bait: bites come 30-40% sooner. Glow Bait draws the night and chemical fish. The legendaries take nothing but Prime Bait.
    • Other catches: a Sunken Crate on 2 / 4 / 6% of casts (near / mid / far), and junk on 12% of the rest.
    • Fish Journal: type /fish in chat.

    Rods¶

    Rod How to get it Hook window Sweet spot Tension climb
    Fishing Rod (tier 1) Workbench or Craftbot. Known from the start. 1.2 s 34% wide (38-72%) 0.55 per second
    Carbon Rod (tier 2) Craftbot, once the party owns the Carbon Rod blueprint from the Traveling Trader 1.6 s 44% wide (33-77%) 0.42 per second
    Frontier Rod (tier 3) Can't be crafted. Paid out by tier 3 and higher Catch Orders. 2.0 s 52% wide (29-81%) 0.34 per second

    What the stats mean:

    • Hook window: how long you have to click after a bite before the fish gets away.
    • Sweet spot: the band on the line-tension bar where reeling works best. It is always centred on 55% tension, and a better rod widens it.
    • Tension climb: how fast the line tightens while you hold the reel. The fish scales it: a hard-fighting fish tightens the line faster. A lower number gives you more time before the line snaps.

    A better rod also brings bites a little sooner: the wait is 10% shorter with the Carbon Rod and 20% shorter with the Frontier Rod.

    Recipes¶

    Rod Where Ingredients Craft time
    Fishing Rod Workbench or Craftbot 8 Scrap Wood Block, 2 Scrap Metal Block, 2 Cotton 30 s
    Carbon Rod Craftbot (needs the blueprint) 6 Metal Block Level 2, 2 Component Kit, 4 Cotton, 1 Circuit Board 60 s

    The Carbon Rod is an Uncommon blueprint. The trader sells it for 72 Frontier Scrip once the party reaches the trader's second reputation level (60 scrip spent in total). A blueprint unlocks the recipe for the whole party. See Traveling Trader.

    The Frontier Rod has no recipe. Catch Orders of tier 3 and higher pay one, until someone in the party claims it. After that, new Catch Orders stop paying it. See Catch Orders.

    Which fish a rod can land¶

    Every fish has a minimum rod: tier 1 (any rod), tier 2 (Carbon Rod or better) or tier 3 (Frontier Rod). A fish that needs a better rod than yours can still bite, but 70% less often than it otherwise would. When you hook it, the line snaps straight away: "Something huge snapped your line! You need a stronger rod." Your bait is used up.

    How to fish¶

    Default controls: Create (left mouse button) does everything.

    1. Cast. Hold the rod in your hotbar, then hold Create to wind up. A full wind-up takes 2.2 s, and a longer wind-up throws further: from 6 m up to 20 m. While you wind up, a preview bobber floats where the cast will land, and the action line shows the distance and the kind of water (for example 14 m - chemical). It shows No water there if the spot is dry. Let go to throw.
    2. Wait. The bobber has to land in water. If it lands on dry ground you get "No water there - cast into a lake, pond or pool" and nothing happens. A bite comes after 5-25 seconds (shorter with bait and a better rod, see below). If a fish is on its way, its shadow circles the bobber and closes in as the bite gets near. No shadow means junk or a Sunken Crate.
    3. Tease (optional). While you wait, each fresh click of Create cuts the time left before a bite to 75%, up to 4 times. Holding Create reels the bobber in at 1.5 m/s. If you reel it to within 3 m of you, or onto the shore, the cast ends.
    4. Hook the bite. The bobber splashes and you get "Bite! Click to hook it!". Click Create within your rod's hook window (1.2 / 1.6 / 2.0 s). If you are too slow, it gets away. A missed bite doesn't use bait.
    5. Reel it in. Everything you hook fights, even small fish and junk. See The reeling fight.

    The cast is called off if you put the rod away or walk more than 32 m from your bobber.

    Bite wait times¶

    The wait is a random 5-25 seconds. Bait and the rod tier multiply it:

    No bait Basic / Glow Bait (x0.7) Prime Bait (x0.6)
    Fishing Rod 5-25 s 3.5-17.5 s 3-15 s
    Carbon Rod (x0.9) 4.5-22.5 s 3.2-15.8 s 2.7-13.5 s
    Frontier Rod (x0.8) 4-20 s 2.8-14 s 2.4-12 s

    Teasing shortens these further.

    The reeling fight¶

    Once a fish is hooked, a compact reeling panel appears just above your hotbar. Your movement is slowed while you fight. The panel has three bars:

    Bar Shows Colours
    LINE Line tension, with your rod's sweet spot shaded yellow when slack, green in the sweet spot, orange above it, red at 90% and up
    FISH The fish's strength (stamina) orange, turning green once it's tired (below 25%) or exhausted. Reads JUNK or HEAVY when you've snagged junk or a crate.
    REEL How far it's been reeled in fills up; full means it's landed

    Under the panel, the action line tells you what to do: Hold to reel, It's about to run - stop reeling!, Let go - it's running!, Line's too tight - let go!, Slack line - hold to reel, It's exhausted - hold to reel it in.

    How it works:

    • Hold Create to reel. Reeling pulls the fish in and raises the tension. Letting go drops the tension by 50% of the bar per second. Every fight starts at 35% tension with the fish 8% of the way in.
    • Keep the tension at or above the sweet spot to tire the fish. That drains the fish's stamina, and a tired fish comes in faster: a fresh fish gives only 30% of the normal reel speed, an exhausted one gives all of it. Reeling while the tension is inside the sweet spot pulls in 1.8 times faster. With the line slack (below the sweet spot), the fish gets its breath back.
    • Runs. Every so often the fish makes a run. First it thrashes and splashes for 0.6 s ("It's about to run - stop reeling!"), then it pulls. Let go during a run. The drag gives it line and holds the tension steady, at 50% plus up to 30% more for the hardest fighters. If you reel against a run, the tension spikes fast. Either way you lose some ground during a run, and it jolts the camera and drags you towards the water. Runs come at least 1.5 s apart, and a fresh, strong fish runs more often and for longer.
    • Tugs. Between runs the fish jerks the line now and then, which kicks the tension up a little.
    • Exhausted. When its stamina is nearly gone (2%), the fish stops running and tugging. The tension can't climb past the top of the sweet spot, and it comes in 1.5 times faster. Let the line go slack for too long and it recovers: once it's back to 15% stamina, it fights again.

    How a fight ends:

    Result When
    Landed The REEL bar fills. The catch goes into your inventory, or drops at your feet if your inventory is full.
    The line snapped! Tension stays at the very top for 0.3 s.
    It got away... The line stays slack (5% tension or less) for 2.5 s.

    Watch the line

    Holding the reel down the whole time doesn't win. The tension keeps climbing until the line snaps, much faster against a run. Reel between runs, ease off when the bar turns orange or red, and let go the moment the fish runs.

    How hard each catch pulls: small fish pull lightly (strength 0.2), and junk and crates are dead weight (0.1, with no runs and no stamina). The big fish each have their own strength, from 0.45 (Tarback Catfish) up to 0.9 (Crude Leviathan), shown in the fish table. A Sunken Crate is very heavy: its tension climbs 1.6 times faster and it comes in at 55% of the normal speed. Feather the reel.

    Note

    Land your catch within 2 minutes of the bite. After that the catch is no longer accepted.

    Water types¶

    When you cast, the game checks the spot straight down from where the bobber will land. It has to be one of the world's water areas, and the kind of water decides which fish and junk you can catch there:

    Water Where Fish
    Water Lakes and ponds Pond Perch, Mud Carp, Moonfin, Sunscale Trout, Ironjaw Pike, Frontier Sturgeon
    Chemical Chemical pools Glowgill, Sludge Eel, Three-Eyed Tetra
    Crude oil Oil pools Slick Minnow, Tarback Catfish, Crude Leviathan

    The splashes and the sounds of a running fish match the water you're in.

    Distance from the crash site¶

    The distance is measured flat across the map, from the crash site (the start area) to your bobber.

    Band Distance
    Near under 600 m
    Mid 600 m to 2,500 m
    Far 2,500 m and more

    A fish's band is the closest band it lives in. It also bites in every band further out. A Pond Perch bites anywhere, a Sunscale Trout bites in the mid and far bands, and an Ironjaw Pike only in the far band. The band also sets the Sunken Crate chance (see Junk and Sunken Crates).

    Time of day¶

    Window In-game clock (about)
    Night 21:00 to 05:00
    Dawn 05:00 to 07:12
    Day 07:12 to 18:00
    Dusk 18:00 to 21:00

    Fish marked any bite at all hours. Moonfin, Sludge Eel and Crude Leviathan bite only at night. Sunscale Trout bites only by day. Frontier Sturgeon bites only at dawn and dusk.

    Bait¶

    Bait stays in your inventory. You don't hold it. Each cast uses the most useful bait you're carrying for that spot:

    1. Glow Bait if a fish that loves it can bite here, or Prime Bait if a legendary can bite here and you're holding a Frontier Rod.
    2. Otherwise Basic Bait.
    3. Otherwise whatever bait you have.

    One bait is used each time you land something (fish, junk or crate) or the line snaps. A missed bite or a fish that gets away costs nothing.

    Bait Recipe (Workbench) Bite wait Special
    Basic Bait 1 Carrot + 1 Corn → 4 (10 s) x0.7 (30% sooner) none
    Glow Bait 1 Glowgill + 1 Corn → 4 (10 s) x0.7 (30% sooner) Moonfin bites 2.5x, Sludge Eel 2x and Three-Eyed Tetra 3x as often
    Prime Bait 3 Fish Fillet + 1 Chili Pepper → 2 (15 s) x0.6 (40% sooner) Required for the Frontier Sturgeon and Crude Leviathan

    All three bait recipes are known from the start. Catch Orders also pay bait (see below).

    Legendaries need both

    The Frontier Sturgeon and Crude Leviathan never bite without Prime Bait. With Prime Bait but a weaker rod they can bite, but they snap the line every time. Bring a Frontier Rod and Prime Bait.

    The 12 fish¶

    Commonness is how often a fish bites compared with the other fish that can bite in the same spot. For example, in a near lake by day only the Pond Perch (100) and the Mud Carp (90) can bite, so the Perch comes up 100 times in 190.

    Fish Water Closest band When Min rod Commonness Weight Fillets Fight
    Pond Perch Water Near Any Fishing Rod 100 0.2-0.9 kg 1 small
    Mud Carp Water Near Any Fishing Rod 90 0.8-3 kg 2 small
    Moonfin Water Near Night Fishing Rod 45 0.3-1.2 kg 1 small
    Sunscale Trout Water Mid Day Fishing Rod 55 0.6-2.4 kg 2 small
    Ironjaw Pike Water Far Any Carbon Rod 30 4-14 kg 4 big (0.55)
    Frontier Sturgeon (legendary) Water Far Dawn and dusk Frontier Rod + Prime Bait 6 25-80 kg 10 big (0.85)
    Glowgill Chemical Near Any Fishing Rod 100 0.2-1 kg 1 small
    Sludge Eel Chemical Near Night Carbon Rod 35 2-9 kg 3 big (0.5)
    Three-Eyed Tetra Chemical Far Any Fishing Rod 20 0.1-0.4 kg 1 small
    Slick Minnow Crude oil Near Any Fishing Rod 100 0.1-0.5 kg 1 small
    Tarback Catfish Crude oil Mid Any Carbon Rod 50 3-12 kg 4 big (0.45)
    Crude Leviathan (legendary) Crude oil Far Night Frontier Rod + Prime Bait 5 40-120 kg 12 big (0.9)

    Each fish's weight is rolled within its range, rounded to 0.1 kg. Heavy fish are rarer than light ones: a fish lands in the top half of its range only about 3 times in 10. See the full fish reference for every fish's details.

    Odds for the legendaries

    In a far lake at dawn or dusk, with a Frontier Rod and Prime Bait, the fish that can bite are the Pond Perch (100), Mud Carp (90), Ironjaw Pike (30) and Frontier Sturgeon (6). That makes the Sturgeon about 1 fish bite in 38. In far crude oil at night it's the Slick Minnow (100), Tarback Catfish (50) and Crude Leviathan (5), so the Leviathan is about 1 bite in 31. Crates and junk come up on top of that.

    Moonfin hunting

    In a near lake at night with Glow Bait, the Moonfin's 45 becomes 112.5 against the Perch's 100 and the Carp's 90, so about 37% of your fish are Moonfins. Without Glow Bait it's about 19%.

    Junk and Sunken Crates¶

    Every cast first rolls for a Sunken Crate, then for junk, and otherwise it's a fish.

    Sunken Crate¶

    Band Crate chance per cast
    Near 2%
    Mid 4%
    Far 6%

    A crate is heavy dead weight on the line: no runs, but the tension climbs fast. Once landed it breaks open and you get the same loot as a vanilla epic loot crate. Crates landed in the far band also have a 5% chance to hold a Backpack Expansion (see Backpack). Anything that doesn't fit in your inventory drops at your feet. The Fish Journal counts the crates the party has hauled up.

    Junk¶

    If no crate comes up, 12% of casts snag junk instead of a fish. What you get depends on the water:

    Water Junk (amount, share of junk catches)
    Water Scrap Metal Block 1-3 (42%), Scrap Wood Block 1-4 (42%), Component Kit 1 (12.5%), Circuit Board 1 (4%)
    Chemical Chemical 1-3 (51%), Scrap Metal Block 1-3 (31%), Component Kit 1 (12%), Circuit Board 1 (6%)
    Crude oil Crude Oil 1-3 (54.5%), Scrap Metal Block 1-3 (30%), Component Kit 1 (10%), Circuit Board 1 (6%)

    Junk is dead weight: no runs and no stamina, so just reel it in and ease off if the line gets tight.

    Fish Fillets¶

    Fish Fillet: put a fish in your hotbar, select it and hold Create for 1.5 seconds. That one fish becomes its fillets: 1 for the smallest fish, up to 12 for a Crude Leviathan (see the table above). Letting go early cancels. If there isn't room in your inventory for the fillets, nothing happens ("No room for the fillets").

    Fillets are used in:

    • Prime Bait at the Workbench (3 fillets + 1 Chili Pepper → 2).
    • Grilled Fish at the Campfire (2 fillets), plus several fish dishes at the Cookbot, such as Fish Chowder. Some dishes, such as Smoked Pike, take a whole fish. See Frontier Kitchen and the recipe list.

    Fish Journal and party records¶

    Type /fish in chat to open the Fish Journal (see Commands). It shows:

    • A summary: how many of the 12 species the party has found, the total fish caught by the party, and how many Sunken Crates were hauled up.
    • Every species. Fish you haven't caught show as dark silhouettes marked ???, with a hint about where and when to look and what rod and bait they need. Caught fish show how many the party has landed and the record weight, with the name of the player who holds it. Legendaries are named in gold.

    The records belong to the whole party and are saved with the world. Chat announces each first catch of a species ("Name caught the party's first Moonfin (0.8 kg)!") and each new record ("New Moonfin record: 1.1 kg by Name (was 0.8 kg)"). Legendary catches are prefixed LEGENDARY!

    Catch Orders¶

    Catch Orders on the Contract Board ask the party to hand in fish, the same way as a supply run. They pay Frontier Scrip and materials like other contracts, plus bait:

    Order tier Asks for (one per order) Bait paid
    1 6 Lake Fish (any), 4 Glowgills or 4 Slick Minnows 8 Basic Bait
    2 3 Moonfins, 3 Sunscale Trout, 6 Mud Carp or 6 Glowgills 6 Glow Bait
    3 1 Ironjaw Pike, 2 Sludge Eels or 2 Tarback Catfish 8 Glow Bait + a Frontier Rod*
    4 2 Ironjaw Pike, 1 Three-Eyed Tetra or 2 Tarback Catfish 4 Prime Bait + a Frontier Rod*
    5 2 Three-Eyed Tetras, 3 Big Fish (any), 1 Frontier Sturgeon† or 1 Crude Leviathan† 6 Prime Bait + a Frontier Rod*

    * Only until someone in the party claims a Frontier Rod. † Only offered once the party has a Frontier Rod.

    From tier 3 up, most Catch Orders need big fish, so get a Carbon Rod first. See Contracts for how tiers, Frontier Standing and rewards work.
